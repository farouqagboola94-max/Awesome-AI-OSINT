ṢÀNGÓ'S DAUGHTER
86 Pages · Free · Expanding toward 100
Panel 1 — Flashback · Mushin, 11 Years Earlier
What the Drum Remembers
Amara Okafor is eight years old and her grandfather is teaching her that the talking drum is not a percussion instrument. It is a telephone. The oldest one in existence.

"Every message has a receiver," Chief Taiwo Okafor tells her, his hands guiding hers on the taut skin of the ìyá'lù. "Most people think we are speaking to each other. We are not. We are speaking to the one who listens to everything." He taps twice — short-long-short. "That pattern. Say it until your hands know it without your mind."

She practices it for three years. Her grandfather dies. She keeps practicing. She is speaking, she finally understands at fourteen, not to other humans. She is speaking to Ṣàngó — and Ṣàngó has been answering all along in the static of every Lagos thunderstorm.

PANEL 1B — Present · National Theatre plaza, 6:45 AM
The Other Reason She Fights
VISUAL: Amara busking at the base of the National Theatre steps, the ìyá'lù slung low, a scattering of coins and two crumpled two-hundred-naira notes in the drum's velvet-lined case. She has done this every morning for four years, rain or dry season, before the office crowd arrives and the tips get generous around lunchtime.

She sends most of it — not all, but most, the discipline of someone who learned early that "most" is sustainable and "all" is a story that ends badly — to Enugu, to the small flat where her grandmother, Mama Ngozi, is losing a slow argument with her knees and refuses, on principle, to stop cooking for the whole compound anyway.

MAMA NGOZI (voice note, played back through Amara's cracked phone speaker while she counts the morning's coins) —
Amara, you sent too much again this month. Keep something for yourself. A drummer needs good shoes, not just a good drum.

Amara has exactly one pair of shoes, and they are not good. She smiles at the voice note anyway, the specific smile people save for the one relative who still worries about them the ordinary way, instead of the way everyone in Lagos has started worrying about her since the Balogun Nexus footage went online.

VISUAL: Her phone, mid-scroll — a grainy citizen video of the market wreckage, 340,000 views overnight, comment section split evenly between "Federal Government don finally test weapon for us city" and "na Yoruba gods dey fight for Lagos again, everybody stay indoor." Amara reads exactly one comment thread before putting the phone face-down. She has thunder in her hands and rent in Enugu to think about. Today those two facts are, uncomfortably, the same fact.
Panel 2 — Present · Balogun Nexus, Aftermath
The Collector Arrives

The Pale Council's first named operative lands in Lagos on a commercial flight from Geneva. He books a suite at Eko Hotel, orders room service, and spends the evening reviewing Orisha energy readings. His name — the one on the passport, anyway — is Dr. Emmanuel Adaeze. His function: harvest Orisha resonance from active vessels before it stabilises. The Pale Council calls this "collection." The Orishas call it something older and less clinical.

THE COLLECTOR (internal monologue) —
Three active vessels in a single city. Unprecedented. But the readings on the fourth signal — the un-classified one — those are... those aren't possible. The instruments must be wrong.

The instruments are not wrong.

Panel 3 — The First Team Fight
Four People Who Don't Trust Each Other, Trying Not to Die

The Collector doesn't fight directly. He harvests from a distance, using Ajele as proximity relays. Thunderstrike loses thirty percent of her drum's resonance before she realizes what's happening. Iron Wolf's armor begins to rust — in real time, visibly, his plates flaking orange under a Lagos afternoon sun.

Bayo can feel it too. Something pulling at something inside him. Like a low-pressure system in his chest. He reaches out with the thing he still doesn't have a name for—

[ ASẸ SYSTEM — FIRST ABILITY IDENTIFICATION ]
VESSEL: ADEBAYO ADEYEMI
NEW ABILITY DETECTED: ẸṢÙ'S CROSSROADS
→ See all possible paths through any situation simultaneously
→ Duration: 12 seconds. Cost: significant disorientation
USE NOW? [Y/N] (You have 3 seconds to decide)

Bayo sees 847 possible futures unfold in three seconds. In 846 of them, one of his team dies. In the one where no one dies, he does something that makes no logical sense. He walks directly toward The Collector.

BAYO —
Stop the harvest. I'm giving you something better to think about.

The Collector — who has neutralised twelve Orisha vessels across nine countries — looks at this homeless teenager from Mushin and feels, for the first time in his career, genuinely uncertain about what he is looking at.

PANEL 3B — Simultaneous, ground level · what the harvest actually felt like
Rust and Static
VISUAL: While Bayo walks his single surviving path toward the Collector, the other three are living through the eleven seconds it takes him to get there — which, from inside a harvesting field, does not feel like eleven seconds.

Ikenna feels it as cold first, then as absence. The iron plating that has been a second skin for three years — that has never once asked his permission before flexing, striking, catching — goes quiet under his hands, like a limb falling asleep from the inside. He watches, at arm's length from his own body, a patch above his left elbow bloom into fine orange rust in real time. It itches. That is somehow the worst part. Not pain. An itch, exactly like corrosion, because it is exactly corrosion, happening to him, right now, in daylight.

IKENNA (through gritted teeth) —
Whatever this is — it's not taking my strength. It's taking my time. Ageing the metal on purpose.

Zara, forty metres back on a rooftop she found in under ninety seconds, is not being harvested — Ẹṣù's subtraction-gift apparently reads as noise to whatever instrument the Collector is using, a fact she files away with the specific coldness of someone who has just learned her power might be the one thing in this fight immune to the enemy's whole strategy. She uses the ninety seconds of clarity to do the only thing she is actually built for.

ZARA (into the compound-linked earpiece, calm, cataloguing) —
Device is a tuning fork rig, forearm-length, silver housing, no visible power source — meaning it's running on someone else's Aṣẹ, not a battery. Range looks like fifteen, twenty metres. He needs line of sight and stillness. He has neither once Bayo starts moving.

Amara, mid-harvest, hears her own drum go quiet under her palms for the first time in eleven years — not broken, worse, ignored, Ṣàngó's line gone to a dial tone — and understands, with a clarity that will stay with her long after today, exactly how much of her identity she has built on a connection she just learned could be switched off by a stranger with a tuning fork and a briefcase.

AMARA (to no one, very quietly) —
Eleven years. Eleven years I thought that line was mine to lose, not his to take.
PANEL 3C — Flashback interstitial · A face-me-I-face-you room, Mushin · the night before
What Ikenna Doesn't Tell the Team
VISUAL: A single room shared by Ikenna and his older sister Chidinma, who works the overnight shift at a Lekki call centre and sleeps through most of the daylight hours he's awake. Their parents are both alive, both in Onitsha, both under the impression Ikenna is "doing well for himself" at the workshop — a phrase that has quietly come to mean something different from what it used to.

He does not tell his sister about the metal moving in his hands before his hands ask it to. He has been not-telling her for seven months, the same seven months the pattern has been building in his sleep. It is easier, he has decided, to be the brother who fixes danfo engines than the brother who might be something the family has no framework for.

CHIDINMA (half-asleep, not really expecting an answer) —
You dey shake the whole bed. Bad dream?
IKENNA —
Something like that.

He does not tell her that the dream is always the same: four hundred and sixteen objects turning toward a threat that, until this morning, existed only in his sleep. He does not tell her that after tomorrow, he suspects he will not be able to keep not-telling her much longer. Some things, once they happen in daylight, stop agreeing to stay quiet at night.

PANEL 4 — Technical breakdown sequence · Amara's workshop, Mushin · 6:00 AM
The Drum Code Table
VISUAL: Close on the talking drum — the ìyá'lù — suspended on a rack in Amara's workshop. The cracked drumhead repaired overnight with something that is not standard hide. The marks her grandfather painted on it forty years ago are still present underneath her own additions. The room smells of resin and harmattan dust and something electrical that has no origin in physical chemistry.
AMARA (to the rest of the team, who she is slowly deciding to trust) —
The drum doesn't make thunder. It tunes to Ṣàngó's frequency and requests it. There's a difference. The request has to be grammatically correct in the old Yoruba tonal system — one wrong pitch and you're not calling Ṣàngó, you're calling whoever else is on that frequency.
BAYO —
Who else is on that frequency?
AMARA (quiet) —
Things my grandfather told me never to call.
VISUAL: The drum code table on the workshop wall — eight rows of tonal patterns, each labelled in old Yoruba script. The Collector's energy harvesting had targeted the drum specifically because the drum is not just a weapon. It is a communication channel. Harvesting from it is like tapping a live telephone wire to a god.
[ ASẸ SYSTEM — VESSEL PROFILE: AMARA OKAFOR ]
MID: OKF·22·Λ:SAN-A·TIER:III·CLASS:CONDUIT·FLAG:ÌLÙMỌ̀NÀ
ṢÀNGÓ RESONANCE: 94 / 100 · CONDUIT EFFICIENCY: 87%
ABILITY: Tonal Frequency Modulation — tune Ṣàngó's thunder to any wavelength
ABILITY: Concussive Resonance — directed thunder at frequencies that affect neural function
NOTE: When drum resonance drops below 60%, vessel loses access to Ṣàngó-channel entirely. The Collector knew this. That was why he targeted the drum.
PANEL 5 — Clinical horror sequence · Collector's field operation, somewhere in Lagos Island · 11:15 AM
How the Harvesting Actually Works
VISUAL: The Collector's equipment — not weapons. Medical instruments. Recalibrated EEG equipment, modified signal detectors, a device that looks like a tuning fork the length of a forearm. These are the tools of someone who regards Orisha power as a resource to be extracted, processed, and sold. The clinical calm of it is more frightening than any weapon would be.
THE COLLECTOR (his report, read aloud in voice-over) —
Aṣẹ resonance extraction proceeds as follows: identify the vessel's primary Orisha frequency — each has a specific wavelength, as distinct as a fingerprint. Apply counter-frequency at the correct proximity. The resonance does not leave the vessel violently. It is drawn out slowly, like pressure equalising. The vessel feels tired. Then confused. Then empty. The process takes between eight and forty minutes depending on the vessel's tier. The material extracted is stored in sealed quantum-resonance containers for transport.
VISUAL: Cross-section diagram of the harvesting process — rendered in the Pale Council's clinical blue-white style — showing the resonance being pulled from a generic vessel outline into the containment device. The diagram is beautiful in the way weapons diagrams are always beautiful. Technically precise. Morally empty.
THE COLLECTOR (continuing) —
The Lagos anomaly — Vessel ADY-19 — resists this process entirely. Counter-frequency application resulted in the counter-frequency being absorbed, amplified, and returned at ten-times-magnitude. This has never been recorded. The vessel appears to function not as a container for Aṣẹ but as a — processor. What enters is not what exits. Classification: impossible with current taxonomy. I am requesting the Architect.
PANEL 6 — Six-panel sequence · Balogun area, moving north · 11:42 AM
The One Path That Works
VISUAL: Six panels, rapid cuts — Bayo moving through the Balogun crowd, one step per panel, his expression shifting from uncertain to resolved. The crowd moves normally around him. No one knows. He is indistinguishable from any other nineteen-year-old in Lagos. That is the advantage.
The Ẹṣù ability — seeing all 847 paths simultaneously — is still new enough to be disorienting. The paths don't present as visions. They present as certainties: absolute knowledge of how each decision chain resolves. Walking directly toward the Collector is the one path where no one dies. The other 846 paths have varying numbers of casualties. The range is: one to all four of them.
BAYO (internal, per-panel captions) —
Path 001: I wait. Amara loses more resonance. She can't tune the drum for six weeks. In that six weeks, the Collector harvests Ikenna. Loss: two team members, temporarily. Path 847: I walk toward him. He has to stop the harvest to deal with me. In the two seconds he redirects attention, the drum recovers to 62%. Amara fires. I absorb the blowback. Nobody dies. Cost to me: significant. What counts as significant now is different from what it used to be.
VISUAL FINAL PANEL: Bayo emerging from the crowd into the empty side street where the Collector is operating. He is completely calm. The calm is new. He doesn't know yet that this is what the Aṣẹ System calls Ẹṣù's gift — the emotional flatness that comes from already knowing the outcome. It looks like confidence. It isn't. It's something stranger.
PANEL 6B — Montage · Lagos, 1:00 PM to 3:00 PM
What the City Decided to Believe
VISUAL, newsroom-collage layout: Six competing versions of "what happened on Awolowo Road" spreading across Lagos in the two hours after the Collector's retreat, none of them accurate, all of them, in their own way, closer to true than the official silence that follows.
CHANNELS TV, MID-BULLETIN —
— authorities have declined to comment on reports of a "localised electromagnetic event" affecting three blocks near Balogun for the second time this week. LASEMA describes the incident as "under investigation."
A DANFO CONDUCTOR, TO A FULL BUS, UNPROMPTED —
I dey tell you since — na Ṣàngó dey vex for Lagos. Since when the government fit explain am for us like network downtime?
A TWITTER THREAD, 40,000 QUOTE-TWEETS, TOP REPLY —
my aunty for Isolo say she see one woman drumming for the market fit stop bullet with sound. nobody believe her. nobody believing anybody sha, but everybody indoor by 6pm now.
VISUAL: Beneath the noise, a quieter current — three older women outside a Celestial Church in Yaba, none of them famous, none of them online, comparing notes on which Orisha's feast day is closest, agreeing without much discussion that whatever is waking up in Lagos will want proper offerings soon, and that the young ones will not think to give them until it is nearly too late. They are not wrong. They will not be listened to either. This is also, in its way, an old Lagos story.
PANEL 6C — Amara's workshop, 3:20 PM
Four Chairs, One Table, No Noble Speeches
VISUAL: The four of them crowded around a workbench built for one person's tools, sharing a plate of roasted corn and ube someone bought from a stall on the walk over. It is not a war council. It is, more accurately, four young adults processing a genuinely terrible day the way young adults actually do — badly, a little too loudly, with jokes that land wrong before they land right.
IKENNA (examining the rust patch on his forearm, now still) —
Verdict: this will not buff out. I am going to be the only twenty-two-year-old in Lagos with a permanent rust freckle.
AMARA —
Call it a war wound. Women love a war wound.
IKENNA —
Women do not love rust, Amara.
ZARA (not looking up from the tuning-fork schematic she is redrawing from memory) —
Can the two of you be a functioning combat unit for one more minute. This device runs on stolen Aṣẹ, which means somewhere there's a supply chain, and supply chains have weak points.
BAYO (quiet, still turning over the day) —
Eight hundred and forty-seven paths. I don't think I've slept properly since Monday, and I just watched all of them at once in three seconds. Is it always going to be like this?
AMARA (softer, the joke gone out of her voice) —
Probably. But you watched them alone in that alley six days ago. Today you watched them with three people already moving to catch you if one of them went wrong. That part's new too. Don't let the system update overshadow that one.
VISUAL, final: A small, unremarkable moment — Ikenna passing Bayo the last of the roasted corn without being asked, the way you do for someone you have decided, without a formal vote, belongs at the table now. Nobody comments on it. Nobody needs to.
PANEL 6D — Zara, alone, a back room of Amara's workshop · 3:40 PM
Dr. Emmanuel Adaeze, According to Nine Months of Filing Cabinets
VISUAL: Zara has three phones and none of them are registered to her real name. She lays the Collector's cover identity against nine months of Pale Council shell-company filings the way other people lay out a deck of cards, and the pattern that emerges is worse than she expected, which is a genuinely high bar for Zara.
ZARA (subvocal report, recorded for the Oracle) —
"Dr. Emmanuel Adaeze" has entered nine countries in four years under six different names. Every entry precedes an unexplained energy anomaly by two to six weeks. Every anomaly is followed, within a year, by a "resource extraction incident" in the same region — always framed as industrial, mining, agricultural. Never once framed as what it actually is.

She pulls the number that matters most from the Collector's own field report, the one line that had made even the Oracle go quiet: twelve Orisha vessels across nine countries. Twelve people. Not concepts. Not "vessel classifications." Twelve specific human beings, somewhere, who woke up to their own gift the same way Bayo did six days ago, and who are not walking around free today.

ZARA (to herself, then, deliberately, into the record) —
Twelve. Not twelve incidents. Twelve people who are still somewhere, being kept as something. I want to know where "somewhere" is before this file gets buried under whatever the Architect brings with her.
PANEL 6E — Compound courtyard, dusk · the black card, revisited
Twelve, and One
VISUAL: The Oracle sets Zara's report beside the black card from the night before — the one that, in Bayo's hand, briefly resolved a stranger's name in dying Yoruba script before shutting like a door on someone still screaming. He has not needed thirty-seven years of patience to see the shape this makes. He has needed about four seconds.
THE ORACLE (very still) —
The card draws on a living Aṣẹ signature to stay active. The Collector's own report, filed hours ago, confirms twelve vessels harvested and contained across nine countries. I do not believe these are two separate facts, children. I believe we are holding a key to one of twelve locked doors, and we do not yet know which door, or in what country, or whether the person behind it has any years left to be found in.
BAYO —
Then we don't wait for the Architect to make this bigger. We start looking now.
THE ORACLE —
We start looking now. Carefully — the Council does not misplace its captives by accident, and whatever holds that name captive will not surrender it without noticing who came asking. But yes. Now.
VISUAL, final: The black card, dark and quiet on the table, no longer just a threat delivered under a bridge. A promise, unintentionally made by the people who built it — that somewhere, twelve doors are waiting, and Lagos has just acquired its first key.
PANEL 6F — Full-page finale · National Theatre plaza, well after dark
The Pattern Her Hands Already Know
VISUAL: Amara, alone this time, back at the same spot she busks every morning — except at 9 PM the plaza is empty, the office crowd long gone home, and she is not performing for tips. She has the ìyá'lù across her knees and her grandfather's exact words in her head for the first time in eleven years without the panic underneath them.

"Every message has a receiver," he told her at eight years old, hands guiding hers. "Most people think we are speaking to each other. We are not." Today, for the first time, she understands the sentence completely instead of just remembering it. She has spent eleven years assuming the receiver was Ṣàngó and the message was always the same one — more, louder, now. Today the Collector's tuning fork proved that assumption wrong in the worst possible classroom. A conduit that can only shout in one direction is a conduit an enemy can simply switch off.

VISUAL: Her hands on the drumhead, deliberately slow, none of the urgency of a market under attack. She plays the short-long-short pattern her grandfather taught her — not to summon thunder, not tonight. To ask a question, properly, grammatically, the way he always meant for it to be asked.
[ ASẸ SYSTEM — VESSEL UPDATE: AMARA OKAFOR ]
CONDUIT EFFICIENCY: 87% → 94%
NEW ABILITY: DIRECTED REQUEST — communicate specific intent through tonal pattern, not raw output
NOTE: A conduit that can only receive at full volume can be jammed. A conduit that can modulate its own request cannot — there is no single frequency left to block.
ṢÀNGÓ, DIRECT RESPONSE [logged, unprecedented] —
"Your grandfather asked me for thunder for forty years and I gave him thunder, because that is all he ever requested. Tonight, for the first time in this line, someone asked me for control instead. That is a harder gift to earn and a much harder one to take away. Well asked, daughter."
AMARA (alone, quietly, to a grandfather eleven years gone and a god who just, for the first time, actually answered her by name) —
Eleven years I thought I was learning to shout louder. Turns out I was just learning to finally ask properly. Took a stranger with a tuning fork to teach me the difference. I'll take the lesson. I won't thank him for it.
PANEL 7 — Two-page spread · Aftermath · Awolowo Road, later that night
What the Collector Filed
VISUAL: The Collector's hands, shaking slightly, as he types his final Lagos report at a café table on Awolowo Road, hours after the fact, unable to stop replaying the counter-frequency returning ten-times-magnitude. He has been doing this work for eleven years across nine countries. His hands have never shaken before today.
[ PALE COUNCIL — CLASSIFIED FIELD REPORT · LAGOS OPERATION ]
OPERATIVE: DR. EMMANUEL ADAEZE · DATE: [REDACTED] · CLEARANCE: OMEGA-7
ASSESSMENT: IMMEDIATE ESCALATION REQUIRED
VESSELS IDENTIFIED: 4 · VESSELS SUCCESSFULLY HARVESTED: 0
ANOMALY VESSEL ADY-19: Counter-frequency absorption confirmed × 3 attempts. Each attempt returned amplified. Third attempt generated a localised Aṣẹ-pulse that temporarily disrupted all Pale Council equipment within 200m radius.
CLASSIFIED NOTE: Vessel ADY-19 appears to be aware of multiple future states simultaneously. This is consistent with theoretical ÌMỌ̀LẸ̀-ÀÁRỌ̀ classification. If confirmed — the Ìmọ̀lẹ̀ Protocol is no longer theoretical. It is an active emergency.
RECOMMENDATION: SEND THE ARCHITECT. Immediately. With full resource allocation.
[ I am removing myself from this operation. Not for safety reasons. Because I don't think I am equipped to face what Lagos has produced. That is a statement I have never made in eleven years of field work. ]
VISUAL FINAL PANEL: The Collector's reflection in the café window. Behind his reflection — the Lagos skyline. Somewhere in that skyline, four people are learning to trust each other. He does not know he will never return to Lagos. He does not know that, six arcs from now, his report will be entered as evidence in a tribunal that does not yet exist, for crimes that have not yet been committed. Lagos takes its time. But Lagos always settles its accounts.
End of Issue #02 · Continue in Issue #03
The Collector retreats. The Pale Council receives his report: "Classification impossible. Recommend immediate containment. Send the Architect."
