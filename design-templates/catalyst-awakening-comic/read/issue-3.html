Arc I: The Catalyst · Issue 03
IRON IN THE BLOOD
76 Pages · Free · Expanding toward 100
Panel 1 — Paul Street Mechanic Workshop, Mushin · 6:14 AM
Ikenna and the Iron That Thinks
Ikenna Chukwuemeka has been apprenticing at this workshop for three years. He is twenty-two. He fixes things. He is very good at it. He is less good at explaining why the metal sometimes moves in his hands before his hands tell it to.

The morning of Arc I, Issue 3 begins with Ikenna reassembling a Toyota Hilux engine that a danfo driver brought in "sounding strange." The engine is not sounding strange. The engine is vibrating in a specific pattern that Ikenna has been hearing in his sleep for seven months. The pattern, he will eventually learn, is Ògún's signature frequency in iron.

When the Pale Council's strike team enters the workshop through the rear — six operatives with energy suppressors designed specifically to neutralise Orisha resonance — Ikenna is elbow-deep in the engine bay. He doesn't hear them coming. The iron does.

Every metal object in the workshop turns toward the threat simultaneously. Four hundred and sixteen individual pieces of iron, steel, and alloy, all oriented at the same moment like a compass finding north. Then they move.
Panel 2 — The System Speaks
DAILY QUEST

Bayo arrives at the workshop to find Ikenna standing in the ruins of six suppression units, covered in iron filings, the engine he was working on somehow still intact in the centre of the chaos. The two of them have been moving in parallel orbits since the Balogun Nexus incident. They have not spoken directly.

IKENNA —
I didn't do this on purpose.
BAYO —
Yeah. I know that feeling.
[ ASẸ SYSTEM — DIVINE QUEST RECEIVED ]
ISSUING AUTHORITY: ÒGÚN, LORD OF IRON
QUEST: "IRON TRIAL I OF VII — SURVIVE THE FORGE"
Location: Mushin Central Foundry, 11 PM tonight
Description: Enter the living flame. Let it burn everything false from you.
Duration: Until dawn, or until you break.
REWARD: [UNKNOWN — ÒGÚN DOES NOT NEGOTIATE REWARDS IN ADVANCE]
FAILURE CONDITION: Death
BAYO —
This system wants me to walk into a forge tonight and stay there until morning. Ògún's idea of a fair deal.
IKENNA —
...you too? I got the same quest. I thought I was hallucinating.
Panel 3 — Iron Trial I · 11 PM to Dawn
What the Fire Burns Away

The Mushin Central Foundry has not operated since 2019. The furnace ignites on its own at 11:00 PM to the second. Bayo and Ikenna stand at its mouth. The heat is not natural heat. It is evaluative heat. It reads them, identifies what it wants to burn, and burns specifically and surgically only that.

For Bayo: it burns his fear of being nothing. The deep, constant, background radiation of a life that started hard and kept getting harder. The orphan's knowledge that the world does not notice you missing. The voice that says — even as you do something impossible — it was probably a mistake, it probably won't happen again.

For Ikenna: it burns his control. His careful, precise, responsible management of something he knows could go wrong. The assumption that the iron needs his permission.

By 4 AM, Bayo is not Bayo anymore in any way he would recognise from six hours ago. He is the same person — same calluses, same thirty-two naira, same Mushin address he no longer has access to — but the subtraction has changed the proportions of everything. He is less afraid than he has ever been in his life.

The furnace goes dark at 5:47 AM. Dawn. In the silence after, the Aṣẹ System updates without drama, without fanfare, the way Lagos moves on after something huge — noting it, filing it, continuing.
[ IRON TRIAL I — COMPLETE ]
ÒGÚN RESONANCE: +22 → CURRENT: 83 / 100
NEW ABILITY: IRON SENSE — Perceive metal within 40m radius
TRIALS REMAINING: VI
ÒGÚN'S MESSAGE: "You did not break. This surprises me. Come back tomorrow."
PANEL 4 — Full-page spread · Mushin Central Foundry · Forge Trial II · Second night, 11:00 PM
The Patience Trial
VISUAL: The Foundry from inside — cathedral scale, darkness punctuated only by the single live furnace in the east wall. Bayo and Ikenna stand before it. The difference from Trial I: nothing moves. The furnace is lit. The heat is real. Nothing else happens. No enemy. No challenge. No instructions. Just: the furnace, the heat, and time.
IKENNA (after one hour of silence) —
Is this — are we meant to do something?
BAYO —
The system said: stand in the fire. So we stand in the fire.
IKENNA —
For how long?
BAYO —
Until Ògún decides we've stood long enough.
VISUAL: Time-lapse panel sequence — the same frame, same two figures, the light in the foundry windows moving from black to deep blue to grey. Five hours. Seven hours. Ten hours. Neither of them speaks for the last four. The heat is cataloguing their discipline. Ògún does not reward strength. He rewards the specific quality of will that refuses to be bored into quitting.
At dawn, the furnace goes cold mid-breath. In the silence that follows, Ikenna looks at his hands and finds that the iron-plates that normally require conscious effort to manifest are present without him summoning them. Permanent. Ambient. Ògún has made them part of his resting state.
[ IRON TRIAL II — COMPLETE · THE PATIENCE TRIAL ]
ÒGÚN'S ASSESSMENT: You did not break. You did not ask. You did not hurry the forge. This is the rarest thing among humans.
BAYO — ÒGÚN RESONANCE: +14 → CURRENT: 83 / 100
IKENNA — IRON MANIFESTATION: Now passive · No longer requires activation
TRIALS REMAINING: V
PANEL 4B — Mushin Central Foundry · Forge Trial III · Third night, 11:00 PM
The Trial That Isn't About the Fire
VISUAL: The furnace ignites as always, but tonight it does not burn hot. It burns low, banked, almost gentle — and beside it, laid out on the workshop floor with the care of something staged rather than found, are two objects: a child's shoe, small, one strap broken, and an engine part Ikenna does not immediately recognise but somehow already knows is important.
IKENNA (uneasy, circling the objects without touching either) —
This isn't a trial about strength. This is a trial about choosing.
VISUAL: The foundry's far wall dissolves into vision-smoke, showing two versions of the same moment, seven months from now, that will never both be true. In one, a child in Ajegunle is pulled from beneath a collapsed danfo because a piece of reinforcing iron — one Ikenna is currently rebuilding at the workshop, unpaid, on his own time, because the owner is a widow with no money — held under the weight it was never rated for. In the other, that same iron never gets finished, because tonight Ikenna chose to stay home instead of pulling a second unpaid shift after the forge, and the widow's danfo goes back into service exactly as fragile as it came in.
ÒGÚN (not a voice so much as a pressure, a certainty arriving fully formed) —
I do not test what you can lift. Any smith can lift. I test what you notice is worth lifting when no one is paying you to notice. The iron does not care about your invoices. It cares what you choose to finish.

Ikenna does not need seven months to decide. He decided the day the widow first brought the danfo in and he quoted her nothing, which is its own kind of answer — he only needed, tonight, to understand that the decision had been the trial all along, made weeks before he ever knew Ògún was grading it.

[ IRON TRIAL III — COMPLETE · THE TRIAL OF WHAT YOU FINISH ]
ÒGÚN'S ASSESSMENT: You had already passed this one before you walked in tonight. I simply wanted you to know it.
IKENNA — ÒGÚN RESONANCE: +19 → CURRENT: 91 / 100
NEW ABILITY: FORGE-BOND — repairs made with deliberate care hold at roughly triple standard structural tolerance
TRIALS REMAINING: IV
PANEL 5 — Intimate three-panel sequence · Paul Street Workshop · 7:30 AM
What Iron Remembers
VISUAL PANEL 1: Ikenna's hands on the engine block he was working on when the Pale Council's strike team came. He has returned to it. The engine is still intact — he protected it reflexively in the middle of a six-on-one fight. He didn't think about it. His hands just did it.
VISUAL PANEL 2: His face as he understands what this means. The iron didn't protect the engine because it was valuable. The iron protected the engine because Ikenna cared about it. The iron is not a weapon he carries. It is an extension of what he values. This changes everything about how it works.
IKENNA (to Bayo, who has come for Trial debrief) —
The metal has memory. Not intelligence — memory. It remembers what I've worked on. What I've built. What I've fixed. Every piece of iron I've touched in three years of this workshop is — I can feel all of it. Right now. It's still warm from my hands.
BAYO —
That's — how far?
IKENNA —
Every piece of metal I've ever worked. In this workshop, across Mushin, the Third Mainland Bridge — I held part of that bridge together during a repair job in 2021. I can still feel where my hands were on it. It knows me.
VISUAL FINAL PANEL: Bayo's expression. He is doing the tactical mathematics. Six words:
BAYO —
The Pale Council has iron buildings.
PANEL 5B — The face-me-I-face-you room, Mushin · 8:15 AM, same morning
What Ikenna Finally Tells Her
VISUAL: Chidinma just off her overnight shift, still in her call-centre lanyard, finding her brother sitting at the small kitchen table instead of asleep — which has not happened once in the three years she's shared this room with him. He has clearly been waiting for her.
CHIDINMA (wary, reading his face) —
You've been shaking the bed for seven months and now you're sitting up waiting for me like somebody died. Talk.

He has rehearsed this four different ways on the walk home and discarded all four. What he actually says is none of them — just his palm, flat on the table, and four hundred and sixteen pieces of scrap iron from the workshop's spare bin, which he brought home in a feed sack and has been dreading unpacking, rising an inch off the table and holding there, patient, obedient, entirely his.

IKENNA —
This is what's been happening. Seven months of it. It got worse six days ago, the night of the Balogun thing on the news. I should have told you the day it started. I told myself I was protecting you. I think I was actually just scared of what your face would do.

Her face, for a long moment, does several things at once — fear, disbelief, something that might be pride fighting to get to the front of the queue. What she settles on, in the end, is the thing their mother would have settled on, the thing that has kept this family upright through worse than floating scrap metal.

CHIDINMA (finally) —
Okay. Put the iron down gently, not on my good plates. And then you're going to tell me everything, from the beginning, because I refuse to be the last person in Lagos to understand why my own brother keeps disappearing at eleven at night smelling like a foundry.
VISUAL, final: The iron settling back onto the table, soft as a held breath let go. Ikenna talks until the sun is fully up. Chidinma, for the first time in seven months, does not have to guess what's keeping her brother awake. It doesn't make the danger smaller. It makes it theirs, together, which is a different kind of smaller.
PANEL 6 — Shadow sequence · Lagos, unknown location · 2:00 AM
Zara's Night
VISUAL: A rooftop in Lekki Phase 1. The Mirror — Zara Osei — sitting cross-legged at the rooftop edge, looking down at a Pale Council logistics truck moving through the street below. She has been tracking this truck for nine nights. It runs the same route at the same time with the same crew. She knows the crew's names. She knows their shift patterns. She knows which of them are local and which arrived from Geneva six weeks ago.
Zara's ability is not combat-oriented, but it is not un-channelled either — it draws on the older, quieter aspect of Ògún, the road-clearer rather than the smith, the one who decides what crosses a threshold and what doesn't. People who look at her see what they expect to see — or what she wants them to see — or, at her most precise, what will make them make the exact decision that serves her purpose. The Pale Council calls this ability MIRROR-CLASS. The Aṣẹ System has a different name for it.
[ ASẸ SYSTEM — VESSEL PROFILE: ZARA OSEI ]
MID: OSE·26·Λ:OGU-B·TIER:II·CLASS:SPECULUM·FLAG:OJÚGBÀ-ỌKÀ
ÒGÚN RESONANCE: 88 / 100 · PERCEPTION MANIPULATION: ACTIVE
ABILITY: Mirror Protocol — any observer perceives what The Mirror projects, not what exists
ABILITY: Dissolution — temporarily removes herself from perception; she does not become invisible. She becomes unconsidered.
NOTE: Ikenna and Zara are both Ògún vessels of different aspects — his is the forge, hers is the road. Two people can carry the same Orisha and never look like they belong to the same god at all.
VISUAL FINAL PANEL: The truck below passes directly under the streetlight. For three seconds, the driver — one of the Geneva arrivals — looks directly up at the rooftop where Zara is sitting. He sees nothing. He sees the edge of a building against a dark sky. He looks back at the road. Zara watches him go. Her expression is professionally unreadable. She adds something to her notebook — not what she saw, but what she has decided to do about it.
PANEL 6B — Same rooftop, forty minutes later
What She Decided to Do About It
VISUAL: Zara has followed the truck's route on foot to a loading yard off Freedom Way that doesn't appear on any manifest she's ever pulled — not agricultural, not logistics, not even under one of the eleven shell names she's already catalogued. New. Which, nine months into this, is its own kind of alarm.

She does not go in. Going in is the mistake amateurs make and the mistake she stopped making at sixteen. Instead she reads the yard from its edges — a refrigeration truck that is running far colder than any produce needs, a single guard rotation that changes with military precision instead of civilian shift patterns, and, stencilled small on a shipping crate near the gate, a code she recognises instantly because she has seen its twin once before, warm in Bayo's palm under a bridge: the same numeral format as the black card.

ZARA (subvocal, to the Oracle, careful and quiet) —
I think I found where door number two lives. Not confirmed — I'm not walking in blind on a maybe. But the crate coding matches the card's format exactly, and nobody refrigerates produce to negative fifteen degrees.
THE ORACLE (through the earpiece) —
Then you have done in one night what the Council's entire Lagos operation has not managed in six days — found something they did not want found. Mark the location. Tell no one outside this channel. And Zara — good work. I do not say that as often as I should.
VISUAL, final: Zara alone on the rooftop, the yard's cold-blue security light the only thing marking it from the surrounding dark. Twelve doors, the Oracle said. She has just found the outline of one. She does not yet know what — or who — is on the other side of it. That answer, she suspects, is going to cost considerably more than one night's surveillance.
PANEL 6C — Compound courtyard, the following morning
What Four Different Gifts Look Like Pointed the Same Direction
VISUAL: Zara's yard coordinates pinned to the Oracle's sand diagram alongside a rough sketch of the refrigeration unit. The team gathered around it the way people gather around a plan instead of a mystery — closer, more purposeful, less afraid than they were four days ago.
BAYO —
Ikenna, you said you can feel every piece of iron you've ever worked, across the whole city. Can you feel iron you've never touched?
IKENNA (working it through out loud) —
Not the way I feel my own work. But the Iron Sense from Trial I isn't limited to metal I've touched — forty metres radius, anything ferrous. A refrigeration unit that size has a compressor housing, structural framing, probably rebar in the slab. If I get within forty metres of that yard, I won't just sense the building. I'll sense what's moving inside it that shouldn't be — machinery cycles have a rhythm. A body doesn't move like a compressor.
ZARA —
I can get you within forty metres without a single guard clocking either of us. I already mapped the rotation.
AMARA —
And if it goes wrong, I'm forty seconds out with a drum that now actually listens to me when I ask it something specific. Small mercies.
THE ORACLE (watching four young people accidentally invent a reconnaissance plan around his sand diagram) —
Four days ago you were four strangers who happened to survive the same morning. Listen to yourselves now. This — this coordination, unplanned, unrehearsed — this is worth more to me than any of your individual gifts. Go carefully. Confirm only. No one goes through that gate until we know exactly what's behind it.
VISUAL, final: The four of them splitting to prepare — Ikenna already flexing fingers he's about to ask to feel something forty metres of concrete and steel away, Zara re-checking a rotation she has memorised down to the second, Amara quietly retuning her drum to a register she's never needed before. Not a team assembled by prophecy. A team that built itself, one uncoordinated Tuesday at a time.
PANEL 7 — Two-page technical spread · Eko Atlantic Tower 7 · The Architect's Workshop
The Vaccine — What It Actually Does
VISUAL: The Architect's workspace — not an office, a forge. The aesthetic of the Pale Council is clinical and white, but The Architect's personal workspace is all dark metal and exposed mechanics. On the central table: the Vaccine prototype. It doesn't look like a weapon. It looks like a water treatment component — because that's what it's been engineered to interface with.
THE ARCHITECT (internal monologue, building sequence narration) —
The Orisha connection is not supernatural. It is neurological. Aṣẹ receptors are a physical structure — micro-nodes in the neural architecture that process frequencies outside the standard electromagnetic spectrum. They are present in most humans at trace levels. They are highly developed in vessels. They are the reason certain people can hear the drum in a way others simply cannot, or feel the weight of an ancestor's presence in a room. I am not destroying faith. I am disrupting the hardware that faith runs on. The distinction matters to me. It will matter less to them.
VISUAL: Diagram of the Aṣẹ receptor node — rendered in the Architect's careful technical hand. The Vaccine targets these nodes specifically, coating them with a compound that mimics the structure of the receptor and then permanently fills the binding site. No divine frequency can attach. The connection is not severed — it is permanently occupied by something that has no signal. The spiritual equivalent of a number that has been disconnected. The call goes out. Nothing picks up. After enough unanswered calls, people stop calling.
THE ARCHITECT —
Six weeks. The compound is stable. The delivery mechanism is solved. All that remains is the question of ADY-19. Because if even one Aṣẹ receptor in Lagos is immune to the Vaccine — and from what the Collector's report describes, every one of ADY-19's receptors would be immune — then the plan fails. Not slowly. Immediately. You cannot sever a network that has one terminal still live and connected to something that amplifies everything it touches.
VISUAL FINAL PANEL: The Architect's face — finally shown clearly for the first time in the series. She is young. That is the first surprise. The second: she does not look like a villain. She looks like someone who has done the mathematics and arrived at a conclusion she finds genuinely regrettable. This is the most frightening thing about her.
End of Issue #03 · Continue in Issue #04
Six more trials. And somewhere in Eko Atlantic, The Architect is reviewing the Collector's report and building something that has never been built before.
