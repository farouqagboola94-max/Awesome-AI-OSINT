Arc I: The Catalyst · Issue 04
THE PRICE OF AṢẸ
92 Pages · Free · Expanding toward 100
PANEL 0 — Prologue · the refrigerated yard off Freedom Way · 1:50 AM, two nights before Issue #04 opens
What Was Behind Door Number One
VISUAL: Zara walks Ikenna and Bayo through the guard rotation exactly as mapped — a gap of ninety seconds every eleven minutes, wide enough for three people who know precisely where to stand. Ikenna stops at forty metres, exactly as promised, and goes very still.
IKENNA (barely audible) —
The compressor cycles every ninety seconds. There's a second rhythm underneath it. Slower. Irregular in the specific way nothing mechanical is ever irregular.
BAYO —
A heartbeat.

They get close enough to see the container's interior through a frost-caked viewport the yard's designers never expected anyone to reach: a stasis chamber, humming with the same cold-blue containment technology from the Architect's Tower 7 sketches, and inside it, upright, suspended, a woman who has not aged a visible day since whatever photograph the Pale Council used to catalogue her.

VISUAL: Bayo, without deciding to, presses his palm flat against the frozen glass — the same instinct that moved his feet toward the Collector on the first night, before his brain finished forming the thought. This time the black card's dying flicker of a name doesn't cut off after half a second. It resolves completely, gold Yoruba script blooming fully across his vision for the first time.
[ ASẸ SYSTEM — RESONANCE MATCH CONFIRMED ]
SUBJECT IDENTIFIED: COMFORT IBE · VESSEL OF OYÁ · HELD 3 YEARS, 2 MONTHS
CONTAINMENT: ACTIVE · SUBJECT LIVING · SUBJECT AWARE [intermittently]
RELEASE PROTOCOL: LOCKED — requires Architect-level override key not present at this location
This is one of twelve. The System does not know, yet, whether it is counting up or counting down.

Inside the frost, for exactly one second, Comfort Ibe's eyes open and find Bayo's through the glass — aware, unmistakably aware, in a way stasis should not permit. Then the compressor cycles, the blue light dims, and she is gone again beneath it.

ZARA (already moving them out, voice flat with the effort of staying that way) —
We can't break that lock tonight without an Architect-level key, and we can't fight our way out of a compound this fortified with three people. We mark it. We come back correctly, or we come back with eleven more locations mapped and a plan that doesn't get her killed trying. Either way — she has a name now. That's not nothing. Move.
VISUAL, final: The three of them retreating through the same ninety-second gap, none of them speaking, all of them carrying the same image — a woman's eyes opening for one second inside frozen glass. Bayo does not tell the Oracle everything that night. He tells him enough. The rest he keeps, the way his mother taught him to keep the things that were his to carry.
Panel 1 — The Oracle's Compound, Idumota
The Truth He Has Been Keeping
Chief Adesanya Adewale has been alive for one hundred and forty-four years. The Orishas gave him time in exchange for his eyes — he can see Aṣẹ flowing through the physical world, but he sees everything else through the fog that comes with age that shouldn't be possible. He has been waiting for Adebayo Adeyemi specifically since 1987.

He sits across from Bayo in the compound that smells of ìtò and palm oil and old paper, and he tells him the truth.

THE ORACLE —
You are not a vessel. A vessel holds one thing. You are a — there is no Yoruba word for it, so we use the Ifá term — a Ìmọ̀lẹ̀ Àárọ̀. A Morning Light. The point where multiple divine energies converge and, in converging, are changed.
BAYO —
Changed how?
THE ORACLE —
When the Orishas' power passes through you, it evolves. Ṣàngó's lightning becomes something Ṣàngó himself has never produced. Ògún's iron becomes an alloy that exists in both realms simultaneously. You are not channelling the Orishas' power. You are upgrading it. And this terrifies them. And this delights them. And this is why the Pale Council cannot allow you to reach your full potential.
Panel 2 — Eko Atlantic Tower 7 · Simultaneously
The Orisha Vaccine

The Pale Council's actual plan is not what anyone assumed. They are not simply stealing Orisha power for profit — though they are doing that too, efficiently, at scale. Their terminal objective, as Dr. Adaeze's final report before his removal from the Lagos operation makes clear, is the Orisha Vaccine:

A compound introduced to Lagos's water supply that permanently disrupts the Aṣẹ receptors in the human nervous system. Not killing divinity — more clinical than that. Making the population permanently immune to Orisha influence. No more vessels. No more connection to Ọ̀run. A population that cannot be rallied by divine power, cannot be motivated by ancestral legacy, cannot be reached by anything the Orishas have. A population fully available for a different kind of faith: theirs.

The Vaccine is six weeks from completion.

Panel 3 — The Choice
Control It or Let It Grow Wild

Bayo's power is growing faster than his understanding of it. The Aṣẹ System updates without warning, unlocking capacities he hasn't trained for. The previous night, he accidentally pulled a danfo off the road with a thought. The driver was fine. The danfo was less fine.

BAYO —
Oracle. If I try to control it — slow it down, manage it — will we have time? Before the Vaccine?
THE ORACLE —
No.
BAYO —
And if I let it grow wild and I can't control what I become—
THE ORACLE —
Then Lagos has a different problem. One without a clear solution.
BAYO —
So either I stay small and Lagos loses. Or I grow beyond my own control and Lagos might survive. This is the deal.
THE ORACLE (quietly) —
This has always been the deal. Every person who ever chose to carry something larger than themselves has faced this exact moment. You are not special in being asked. You are special in being able to say yes.

Outside the compound window, across the Lagos Lagoon, the lights of Eko Atlantic shimmer. In Tower 7, something new is powering up. The Architect has arrived. And The Architect has been told: no more gentle approaches.

PANEL 3B — Compound courtyard, minutes later
Thirty-Four Days, Four People
VISUAL: Bayo relays the Oracle's words to the other three in the courtyard — stay small and Lagos loses, grow wild and Lagos might survive. He expects argument. He expects someone to say there has to be a third option. Nobody says that. They have all, in their own ways over the last week, already made a version of this same bet.
IKENNA —
I let iron I didn't fully understand become permanent, ambient, part of what I am, every single night for three trials. I didn't have a guarantee it wouldn't cost me something I couldn't get back. Grow wild is just the word for what we've all already been doing, Bayo. You're the only one who has to do it publicly.
AMARA —
Same. I asked Ṣàngó for control instead of volume with zero idea what he'd take in exchange. He took nothing. He gave me something instead. That's not a guarantee it goes that way for you. It's not nothing, either.
ZARA (quiet, the closest she has come to admitting fear out loud) —
Thirty-four days either way, we're all standing in front of whatever the Architect brings. The only question that actually matters is whether you're standing there alone or with three people who already decided, individually, before you asked, that we're not letting you find out what "grow wild" means by yourself.
VISUAL, final: Bayo, looking at the three of them — a mechanic's apprentice, a busker, a woman who spent a week walking through Pale Council offices unseen — and understanding, more clearly than the Oracle's whole explanation managed, exactly what he actually has thirty-four days to protect.
PANEL 4 — Intelligence brief sequence · The Oracle's compound · 7:00 AM
The Mirror's Report
VISUAL: Zara standing before the team — a first. She has been the most distant, the hardest to read. Now she is presenting. There is a map on the floor of the Oracle's compound, drawn in chalk — Lagos, the surrounding region, and thirty-seven marked locations. Each one a Pale Council asset. Each one catalogued over nine nights of personal surveillance.
ZARA —
Thirty-seven confirmed Pale Council positions in the Lagos metropolitan area. Twelve in Eko Atlantic. Seven along the Third Mainland corridor. Four in Mushin — they are specifically watching this neighbourhood because of both of you. The logistics routes run Geneva → Abuja → Lagos Island every seventy-two hours. The crew swaps are on Tuesdays. The Vaccine components are being assembled in Tower 7's sub-basement, Level B3.
IKENNA —
How did you get all of this?
ZARA —
I was standing in their offices. I attended two of their briefings. I rode in a logistics truck from Abuja to Lagos last Thursday. No one saw me.
ZARA (continues, quietly) —
No one ever sees me unless I let them. That's the point.
VISUAL: The team's faces absorbing this. The Oracle's face — he knew. He has known about Zara's ability for six weeks and has been waiting for her to reveal it in her own time. He is not surprised. He is relieved. She trusted them enough to show them. That, from Zara, is the most meaningful gesture in the room.
PANEL 5 — Horror-register sequence · Eko Atlantic waterfront · 9:00 PM
The Lagoon Vision
VISUAL: Bayo at the edge of the Lagos Lagoon — alone, by choice, for the first time in weeks. The water at night is black and reflective and ancient. He is not here to train. He is here because the Aṣẹ System told him to come. The specific instruction was: "Go to the water. Ẹṣù has something to show you." He has learned not to argue with the System's routing suggestions.
VISUAL: The lagoon surface stills completely — impossible in Lagos, where something is always moving the water. The reflection becomes perfect. And in the perfect reflection, Bayo sees not himself but the city as it would look if the Vaccine had already been administered. Same buildings. Same roads. Same population density. But every golden thread of Aṣẹ-energy that Bayo has learned to see woven through everything — the connections between people and their ancestors, between the city and its Orishas, between one Lagos generation and the next — gone. The city is whole. The city is full. The city is cut loose from everything that made it what it is. It is the most frightening thing Bayo has ever seen. More frightening than any combat he has faced.
BAYO (very quietly) —
This is what they want.
[ ASẸ SYSTEM — ẸṢÙ: VISION COMPLETE ]
This is the future along the highest-probability path if the Vaccine is deployed. Time to deployment: 34 days.
The Orishas' connection to Àiyé does not sever cleanly. It tears. The tearing takes generations to stop being felt.
Every child born in Lagos after Vaccine administration will grow up in a city that has forgotten what it was connected to. They will not know they are missing it. That is the specific cruelty of the plan.
Ẹṣù's note: I showed you this because you need to understand what you are fighting for. Not an abstract future. This future. These specific children.
PANEL 5B — Compound courtyard, 10:15 PM
He Tells Them What He Saw
VISUAL: Bayo back from the lagoon, the other three waiting without having been told to wait — Ikenna sharpening a tool he doesn't need to sharpen, Amara retuning a drum that is already in tune, Zara simply present, which for Zara is its own form of vigil. He tells them what Ẹṣù showed him. He does not soften it.
AMARA (after a long silence) —
My grandfather's voice would just... stop meaning anything. Not gone — I'd still remember the words. I just wouldn't feel him in them anymore.
IKENNA —
Every piece of iron I've ever touched would just be iron again. Metal that doesn't remember anything. I didn't know how much I'd built my whole sense of myself on it remembering, until right now, imagining it not.
ZARA (quiet, uncharacteristically unguarded) —
I spent my whole life being the person nobody sees. If the Vaccine goes in the water, I finally get to stop being invisible on purpose — and it will be because there's nothing left underneath for anyone to actually miss. That's not freedom. That's just a different kind of erasure. I'd rather stay hunted and real than safe and hollow.
VISUAL, final: Four people who understand, in specific and personal terms none of them had access to an hour ago, exactly what thirty-four days actually means. Not an abstraction. A deadline with their own names quietly written somewhere inside it.
PANEL 6 — Confession sequence · The Oracle's compound · 11:00 PM
What the Oracle Has Not Said
VISUAL: Bayo and the Oracle, alone in the compound after the rest of the team has left. The Oracle is making tea. This is what he does when he is about to say something difficult — the ritual of the tea gives him time to arrange the words in the right order. Bayo has learned to recognise this. He waits.
THE ORACLE —
There is something I have not told you. About your mother.
BAYO (very still) —
Tell me.
THE ORACLE —
She was not an ordinary mechanic. She — before you were born, she was under consideration. To be a vessel. Not for Ẹṣù. For Ọṣun. The Orisha of love, of fresh water, of everything that keeps a family alive across generations. She chose — she chose to pass it to her child instead of carrying it herself. She made the offering in 2004. Ọṣun accepted. You were born in 2005.
BAYO —
She knew.
THE ORACLE (gently) —
She knew. She knew from before you could walk. That is why she taught you to fix things before she taught you anything else. She said — I remember exactly — she said: "A boy who knows how to fix things will fix everything."
VISUAL: Bayo's face. This is the moment the series has been building to since page one. Not a power-up, not a battle, not a revelation about the enemy. A revelation about a mother. The Aṣẹ System, reading his Ọṣun resonance spike, quietly updates:
[ ASẸ SYSTEM — RESONANCE UPDATE ]
ỌṢUN RESONANCE: 57 → 79 / 100
SOURCE: Contextual emotional resonance with Ọṣun's domain — love, inheritance, the thread between generations
The System notes: this is the fastest single-session resonance gain in its recorded history. Ọṣun is listening. Ọṣun is — pleased is not the word. Ọṣun is present.

The Oracle leaves him with the tea going cold between them. Bayo sits alone for a while in the room that has become, in six days, the closest thing to a second home he's had since the burial. He takes the thirty-two naira out of his pocket — he still carries it, still hasn't spent it, still isn't entirely sure why — and turns it over once, the way he did on his first night here. A boy who knows how to fix things will fix everything. He thinks about Baba Fọlá's stool leg, finally repaired properly. He thinks that maybe his mother wasn't only talking about engines.

PANEL 7 — Three-page action sequence · Eko Atlantic waterfront · 2:00 AM
The Architect's Opening Move
VISUAL: Three Pale Council containment units — vehicles disguised as logistics trucks — converging on the Oracle's compound simultaneously from three directions. This is not a harvest operation. This is a neutralisation order. The Architect has decided: no more collection, no more analysis. The ADY-19 anomaly needs to be removed.
VISUAL: The trucks stop. Thirty-two operatives in full Aṣẹ-suppression armour — suits engineered to block Orisha frequencies — deploy in formation around the compound's perimeter. The formation is precise, pre-planned, and accounts for every known ability of every known vessel in Lagos. It does not account for one thing.
OPERATIVE LEAD (radio, to Architect) —
Perimeter is set. All angles covered. Proceeding on your mark.
THE ARCHITECT (earpiece) —
Mark.
VISUAL: The moment the perimeter closes, thirty metres of Lagos street simultaneously repaves itself in iron — Paul Street asphalt, Mushin scrap, Third Mainland Bridge repair patches, the engine bay of the Toyota Hilux from the workshop. Every piece of iron Ikenna has ever touched, converging on this block from four directions at once. The operatives' formation breaks before the first of them processes what is happening.
IKENNA (stepping out of the compound, very quiet) —
You're in my city. On my street. Made of my iron. This is not the location you should have chosen.
VISUAL: While Ikenna's iron pins the perimeter in place, Amara is already moving — not attacking, tuning. Eleven suppression suits are broadcasting on a jammed frequency meant to block Orisha resonance from reaching her drum at all. She does not fight the jam. She finds the one gap in it Ògún's converging iron just tore open, and sends a single directed thunderclap through that gap alone — not enough to injure through the armour, precisely calibrated to overload every suit's internal speaker array at once. Eleven operatives go down clutching their ears, not their wounds. Ṣàngó, she has learned, does not always need to hurt to win.
AMARA (not even raising her voice) —
Eleven years I thought I only knew how to shout. Turns out I also know exactly how loud to whisper.
VISUAL: Zara is nowhere and everywhere — the remaining operatives' reports, relayed frantically back to the drone feed, place her simultaneously at three different points of the perimeter, because three different operatives are each convinced, with total sincerity, that they are looking directly at her. None of them are. She is standing, unremarked, beside the operative lead the entire time, close enough to read his radio traffic over his shoulder before he's finished sending it.
ZARA (to the operative lead, who cannot see her, one second before she isn't there anymore) —
Tell the Architect Lagos says no. Tell her I said it, specifically — she'll want to know it was me.
VISUAL: The Architect, watching on a drone feed from Tower 7, sees the formation collapse in eleven seconds. She closes the feed. She opens a new file on her workstation. She begins re-planning. Not in defeat — with information. ADY-19 is not the only anomaly. All four of them are anomalies. The plan to remove them one at a time was always the wrong plan. She needs a new architecture. She begins to build it.
VISUAL FINAL PANEL: Dawn breaking over Lagos. The four of them — Bayo, Amara, Ikenna, Zara — standing in front of the Oracle's compound, thirty-two pieces of suppression armour neatly stacked to one side, no operatives in sight. The Oracle in the doorway behind them, tea in hand. The city waking up around them, exactly as it always does. The generator kicks on at the junction. A danfo honks. Someone's Afrobeats plays from an upstairs window. Lagos, unchanged. Lagos, holding.
[ ASẸ SYSTEM — ARC I COMPLETION ASSESSMENT ]
VESSEL ADY-19 · ADEBAYO ADEYEMI · END OF ARC I
TOTAL ASẸ CAPACITY: 78 / 100 → TIER UNLOCK: RESONANCE CLASS
ORISHAS BOUND: 5 / 401 · MID UPDATED: ADY·19·Λ:ESU-A/OGU-A/SAN-B/OYA-C/OSU-B·TIER:I·CLASS:CATALYST
PALE COUNCIL STATUS: Active. Escalating. Architect now personally leading Lagos operation.
TIME TO VACCINE DEPLOYMENT: 34 DAYS
ẸṢÙ'S FINAL NOTE ON ARC I: "You found your team. You found your reason. Now you need to find the version of yourself that can actually stop what is coming. That version does not yet exist. You have 34 days to build it."
End of Issue #04 — Arc I: The Catalyst Continues
The Architect's power floods every Orisha sensor in Lagos simultaneously. Bayo looks up. Bayo smiles. "Oya. Let's see wetin we made of."
