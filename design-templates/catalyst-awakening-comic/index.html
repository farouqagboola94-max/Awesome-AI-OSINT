THE TWO REALMS

Aye àti Orun — The World and the Beyond

Every Yoruba child is taught that existence runs on two parallel tracks: Àiyé — the physical world of Lagos, concrete and exhaust and hunger and joy — and Ọ̀run — the spirit realm where the Orishas reign, where the dead hold court, and where Aṣẹ — the divine power underlying all things — flows like electricity through a copper wire.

For centuries, the membrane between them held. Prayers went up; blessings came down. Eégun masquerades briefly opened the door on festival days. This was the covenant.

The Pale Council broke the covenant

When The Pale Council — a consortium of tech oligarchs — discovered that Ọ̀run energy could be harvested and converted to data currency, they built Eko Atlantic as a spiritual extraction engine. The towers don't just house offices. They drill down through the Lagos Lagoon floor, through Ọṣun's domain, siphoning Aṣẹ at industrial scale.

The membrane is now in tatters. Ọ̀run bleeds into Àiyé. Spirits walk Balogun Market. Dead ancestors appear at wedding celebrations. And the Orishas — who cannot fully manifest in the physical without human vessels — have begun choosing their champions.

Bayo is the Catalyst. Not a vessel for one Orisha — but the living junction point where all divine energy can be focused, amplified, and directed. He is what the Orishas have been waiting for. And The Pale Council knows it.
