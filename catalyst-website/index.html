The World

A CITY
BETWEEN TWO
WORLDS

Neo-Lagos, 2047. The megacity pulses with 40 million souls, danfo buses hurtling through rain-slicked streets, the sweet smoke of suya cutting through petrol fumes. On the surface — chaos. Beneath it — the Ancient War is rekindling.

The Orun — the Yoruba spirit world — has begun bleeding into the physical. Markets shimmer. The Third Mainland Bridge whispers names at 3 AM. Old women in Mushin see things that aren't there. Or perhaps things that have always been there, finally choosing to be seen.

Into this fracture steps Adebayo "Bayo" Adeyemi — a 19-year-old area boy from Mushin with an overdue NEPA bill, a dead phone, and the ancient power of a Catalyst pulsing through his blood. He didn't ask for any of this. Lagos doesn't care.

Arc I: The Catalyst · Issue 01
AWAKEN, O CITY
48 Pages · Free
Panel 1 — 2:47 AM · Mushin, Lagos
Before Everything
Mushin never actually sleeps. It rests with one eye open. Tonight the open eye has seen something it was not meant to see.

Adebayo Adeyemi — Bayo to anyone who knows him, "that Mushin boy" to anyone who doesn't — is nineteen years old and running. Not from anything dramatic. From four area boys who think he stole a phone charger from a junction stall. He didn't. He was sitting near it. That was enough.

He has thirty-two naira in his pocket. His mother died six weeks ago. He has slept in four different places since the burial. None of them beds.

CAPTION —
Three weeks homeless. Before that: her apprentice mechanic on Paul Street. Before that: her son. In that order — she always said — is how a man should measure himself.

He ducks into an alley behind the old Odeon shell. Dead end. He turns. The four area boys slow, spreading out. They are not in a hurry. He has nowhere to go.

AREA BOY —
Oya, bring the charger. Nobody go touch you.
BAYO —
I no steal anything, my guy. I was just sitting down—

The first punch catches him across the jaw. He goes into the wall. His vision goes white at the edges. And then — the world changes.

It comes from above. Not metaphorically. From directly above the Balogun market, four hundred metres north. A tear. Like fabric. Like reality has a seam and something has found it.
Panel 2 — The Ọ̀run-Bleed
What the City Didn't Know to Fear

The tear in the sky above Balogun Nexus is thirty metres wide and getting larger. Through it: not darkness, not fire — light the wrong colour. A purple-gold that does not exist in any Lagos sunset. The street freezes. Cars stop. Generators cut out in sequence for three blocks in every direction.

From the tear, they fall. The Ajele — spirit-agents of an Orisha whose name no one in the crowd knows yet. They look human enough, until they move. Too fast. Too precise. Like something that learned how to pretend to have a body but hasn't quite committed to the lie.

Thunderstrike arrives first. Amara is good — she is genuinely, terrifyingly capable. Her talking drum fires thunderclaps that shatter concrete. She takes out seven Ajele in ninety seconds. Then the eighth catches her drum mid-beat and absorbs the sound. The silence that follows is louder than the thunder was.

Iron Wolf hits the ground from the overpass — Ikenna's iron skin clanging against the asphalt. He punches through three Ajele with satisfying finality. The fourteenth one lifts him off the ground with one hand and throws him through the air. He lands somewhere over the Third Mainland Bridge. He'll be fine. Eventually.

STREET CAPTION —
The crowd runs. Every direction. The danfo drivers abandon their vehicles. Even the most senior area boy in Balogun, a man who has never retreated from anything in thirty years of Lagos life, is already four streets away and accelerating.
Panel 3 — The Moment
He Doesn't Know Why. He Does It Anyway.

Bayo — who was supposed to be unconscious in an alley — has somehow ended up at the edge of the Balogun Nexus. He cannot explain it later. His feet moved. That's all he knows.

An Ajele turns toward him. Raises what it uses as a hand. Fires a bolt of compressed Aṣẹ — divine energy, raw and unprocessed, the kind that levels walls.

Bayo doesn't move. Not because he is brave. Because something in him is already reaching out before his brain has formed the thought. His right palm comes up, and the bolt hits it, and—

Silence. Then. The bolt comes back. Ten times the size. The Ajele catches it and ceases to exist as a concept. The shockwave clears the entire northern half of Balogun Market of every standing structure that wasn't load-bearing.

Bayo stares at his hand. It is not glowing. It is not special-looking. It is just a hand. A nineteen-year-old mechanic's apprentice's hand, calluses from Paul Street still in the lines of the palm.

BAYO —
...wetin happen?
THE ORACLE (from behind, very quietly) —
It's you. Of all people, of all the souls in this city... e don reach your turn.
End of Issue #01 · Continue in Issue #02
The Oracle's staff glows with all five Orisha colours simultaneously. In six hundred years, he has never seen this before.
Arc I: The Catalyst · Issue 02

Panel 1 — Flashback · Mushin, 11 Years Earlier
What the Drum Remembers
Amara Okafor is eight years old and her grandfather is teaching her that the talking drum is not a percussion instrument. It is a telephone. The oldest one in existence.

"Every message has a receiver," Chief Taiwo Okafor tells her, his hands guiding hers on the taut skin of the ìyá'lù. "Most people think we are speaking to each other. We are not. We are speaking to the one who listens to everything." He taps twice — short-long-short. "That pattern. Say it until your hands know it without your mind."

She practices it for three years. Her grandfather dies. She keeps practicing. She is speaking, she finally understands at fourteen, not to other humans. She is speaking to Ṣàngó — and Ṣàngó has been answering all along in the static of every Lagos thunderstorm.

Panel 2 — Present · Balogun Nexus, Aftermath
The Collector Arrives

The Pale Council's first named operative lands in Lagos on a commercial flight from Geneva. He books a suite at Eko Hotel, orders room service, and spends the evening reviewing Orisha energy readings. His name — the one on the passport, anyway — is Dr. Emmanuel Adaeze. His function: harvest Orisha resonance from active vessels before it stabilises. The Pale Council calls this "collection." The Orishas call it something older and less clinical.

THE COLLECTOR (internal monologue) —
Three active vessels in a single city. Unprecedented. But the readings on the fourth signal — the un-classified one — those are... those aren't possible. The instruments must be wrong.

The instruments are not wrong.

Panel 3 — The First Team Fight
Four People Who Don't Trust Each Other, Trying Not to Die

The Collector doesn't fight directly. He harvests from a distance, using Ajele as proximity relays. Thunderstrike loses thirty percent of her drum's resonance before she realizes what's happening. Iron Wolf's armor begins to rust — in real time, visibly, his plates flaking orange under a Lagos afternoon sun.

Bayo can feel it too. Something pulling at something inside him. Like a low-pressure system in his chest. He reaches out with the thing he still doesn't have a name for—

[ ASẸ SYSTEM — FIRST ABILITY IDENTIFICATION ]
VESSEL: ADEBAYO ADEYEMI
NEW ABILITY DETECTED: ẸṢÙ'S CROSSROADS
→ See all possible paths through any situation simultaneously
→ Duration: 12 seconds. Cost: significant disorientation
USE NOW? [Y/N] (You have 3 seconds to decide)

Bayo sees 847 possible futures unfold in three seconds. In 846 of them, one of his team dies. In the one where no one dies, he does something that makes no logical sense. He walks directly toward The Collector.

BAYO —
Stop the harvest. I'm giving you something better to think about.

The Collector — who has neutralised twelve Orisha vessels across nine countries — looks at this homeless teenager from Mushin and feels, for the first time in his career, genuinely uncertain about what he is looking at.

Panel 1 — Paul Street Mechanic Workshop, Mushin · 6:14 AM
Ikenna and the Iron That Thinks
Ikenna Chukwuemeka has been apprenticing at this workshop for three years. He is twenty-two. He fixes things. He is very good at it. He is less good at explaining why the metal sometimes moves in his hands before his hands tell it to.

The morning of Arc I, Issue 3 begins with Ikenna reassembling a Toyota Hilux engine that a danfo driver brought in "sounding strange." The engine is not sounding strange. The engine is vibrating in a specific pattern that Ikenna has been hearing in his sleep for seven months. The pattern, he will eventually learn, is Ògún's signature frequency in iron.

When the Pale Council's strike team enters the workshop through the rear — six operatives with energy suppressors designed specifically to neutralise Orisha resonance — Ikenna is elbow-deep in the engine bay. He doesn't hear them coming. The iron does.

Every metal object in the workshop turns toward the threat simultaneously. Four hundred and sixteen individual pieces of iron, steel, and alloy, all oriented at the same moment like a compass finding north. Then they move.
Panel 2 — The System Speaks
DAILY QUEST

Bayo arrives at the workshop to find Ikenna standing in the ruins of six suppression units, covered in iron filings, the engine he was working on somehow still intact in the centre of the chaos. The two of them have been moving in parallel orbits since the Balogun Nexus incident. They have not spoken directly.

IKENNA —
I didn't do this on purpose.
BAYO —
Yeah. I know that feeling.
[ ASẸ SYSTEM — DIVINE QUEST RECEIVED ]
ISSUING AUTHORITY: ÒGÚN, LORD OF IRON
QUEST: "IRON TRIAL I OF VII — SURVIVE THE FORGE"
Location: Mushin Central Foundry, 11 PM tonight

REWARD: [UNKNOWN — ÒGÚN DOES NOT NEGOTIATE REWARDS IN ADVANCE]
FAILURE CONDITION: Death
BAYO —
This system wants me to walk into a forge tonight and stay there until morning. Ògún's idea of a fair deal.
IKENNA —
...you too? I got the same quest. I thought I was hallucinating.
Panel 3 — Iron Trial I · 11 PM to Dawn
What the Fire Burns Away

The Mushin Central Foundry has not operated since 2019. The furnace ignites on its own at 11:00 PM to the second. Bayo and Ikenna stand at its mouth. The heat is not natural heat. It is evaluative heat. It reads them, identifies what it wants to burn, and burns specifically and surgically only that.

For Bayo: it burns his fear of being nothing. The deep, constant, background radiation of a life that started hard and kept getting harder. The orphan's knowledge that the world does not notice you missing. The voice that says — even as you do something impossible — it was probably a mistake, it probably won't happen again.

For Ikenna: it burns his control. His careful, precise, responsible management of something he knows could go wrong. The assumption that the iron needs his permission.

By 4 AM, Bayo is not Bayo anymore in any way he would recognise from six hours ago. He is the same person — same calluses, same thirty-two naira, same Mushin address he no longer has access to — but the subtraction has changed the proportions of everything. He is less afraid than he has ever been in his life.

The furnace goes dark at 5:47 AM. Dawn. In the silence after, the Aṣẹ System updates without drama, without fanfare, the way Lagos moves on after something huge — noting it, filing it, continuing.

The Truth He Has Been Keeping
Chief Adesanya Adewale has been alive for one hundred and forty-four years. The Orishas gave him time in exchange for his eyes — he can see Aṣẹ flowing through the physical world, but he sees everything else through the fog that comes with age that shouldn't be possible. He has been waiting for Adebayo Adeyemi specifically since 1987.

He sits across from Bayo in the compound that smells of ìtò and palm oil and old paper, and he tells him the truth.

THE ORACLE —
You are not a vessel. A vessel holds one thing. You are a — there is no Yoruba word for it, so we use the Ifá term — a Ìmọ̀lẹ̀ Àárọ̀. A Morning Light. The point where multiple divine energies converge and, in converging, are changed.
BAYO —
Changed how?
THE ORACLE —
When the Orishas' power passes through you, it evolves. Ṣàngó's lightning becomes something Ṣàngó himself has never produced. Ògún's iron becomes an alloy that exists in both realms simultaneously. You are not channelling the Orishas' power. You are upgrading it. And this terrifies them. And this delights them. And this is why the Pale Council cannot allow you to reach your full potential.
Panel 2 — Eko Atlantic Tower 7 · Simultaneously
The Orisha Vaccine

The Pale Council's actual plan is not what anyone assumed. They are not simply stealing Orisha power for profit — though they are doing that too, efficiently, at scale. Their terminal objective, as Dr. Adaeze's final report before his removal from the Lagos operation makes clear, is the Orisha Vaccine:

A compound introduced to Lagos's water supply that permanently disrupts the Aṣẹ receptors in the human nervous system. Not killing divinity — more clinical than that. Making the population permanently immune to Orisha influence. No more vessels. No more connection to Ọ̀run. A population that cannot be rallied by divine power, cannot be motivated by ancestral legacy, cannot be reached by anything the Orishas have. A population fully available for a different kind of faith: theirs.

The Vaccine is six weeks from completion.

Panel 3 — The Choice
Control It or Let It Grow Wild

Bayo's power is growing faster than his understanding of it. The Aṣẹ System updates without warning, unlocking capacities he hasn't trained for. The previous night, he accidentally pulled a danfo off the road with a thought. The driver was fine. The danfo was less fine.

BAYO —
Oracle. If I try to control it — slow it down, manage it — will we have time? Before the Vaccine?
THE ORACLE —
No.
BAYO —
And if I let it grow wild and I can't control what I become—
THE ORACLE —
Then Lagos has a different problem. One without a clear solution.
BAYO —
So either I stay small and Lagos loses. Or I grow beyond my own control and Lagos might survive. This is the deal.
THE ORACLE (quietly) —
This has always been the deal. Every person who ever chose to carry something larger than themselves has faced this exact moment. You are not special in being asked. You are special in being able to say yes.

Outside the compound window, across the Lagos Lagoon, the lights of Eko Atlantic shimmer. In Tower 7, something new is powering up. The Architect has arrived. And The Architect has been told: no more gentle approaches.

The six tiers of Aṣẹ mastery, maintained by the Council of Ifá since the First Awakening. Most humans never surpass Pulse. Bayo is the only known being to traverse all six in a single lifetime.

THE TWO REALMS

Aye àti Orun — The World and the Beyond

Every Yoruba child is taught that existence runs on two parallel tracks: Àiyé — the physical world of Lagos, concrete and exhaust and hunger and joy — and Ọ̀run — the spirit realm where the Orishas reign, where the dead hold court, and where Aṣẹ — the divine power underlying all things — flows like electricity through a copper wire.

For centuries, the membrane between them held. Prayers went up; blessings came down. Eégun masquerades briefly opened the door on festival days. This was the covenant.

The Pale Council broke the covenant

When The Pale Council — a consortium of tech oligarchs — discovered that Ọ̀run energy could be harvested and converted to data currency, they built Eko Atlantic as a spiritual extraction engine. The towers don't just house offices. They drill down through the Lagos Lagoon floor, through Ọṣun's domain, siphoning Aṣẹ at industrial scale.

The membrane is now in tatters. Ọ̀run bleeds into Àiyé. Spirits walk Balogun Market. Dead ancestors appear at wedding celebrations. And the Orishas — who cannot fully manifest in the physical without human vessels — have begun choosing their champions.

Bayo is the Catalyst. Not a vessel for one Orisha — but the living junction point where all divine energy can be focused, amplified, and directed. He is what the Orishas have been waiting for. And The Pale Council knows it.
